# How Life Works — Glossary

Last updated: 2026-11-04 · Covers: Theme 0 diagnostic

Tags: **[consensus]** textbook view · **[research]** active, unsettled · **[Ball]** Ball's interpretive position · *(Tn)* theme where the term matters most

---

**Activation barrier (activation energy, ΔG‡)** — The energy hump a reaction must get over between starting molecules and products. Rate depends on it exponentially: k ∝ e^(−ΔG‡/k_BT). Enzymes lower it. *(T0)*

**Active site** — The pocket in a folded enzyme whose shape and chemistry fit a particular substrate; where catalysis happens. *(T0, T2)*

**Alternative splicing** — Joining a gene's exons in different combinations, so one gene yields several distinct proteins. Most human multi-exon genes do this. [consensus] *(T1)*

**Amino acid** — The building block of proteins. There are 20 standard ones, differing in their side chains (oily, charged, polar). *(T0)*

**Anfinsen's principle** — A protein's amino-acid sequence determines its folded shape. [consensus] for many proteins, with caveats: some need chaperones, some never fold (see intrinsically disordered protein). *(T0, T2)*

**Antiparallel** — The two strands of DNA run in opposite chemical directions (labeled 5′→3′ and 3′→5′). *(T0)*

**Archaea** — One of the two groups of prokaryotes (the other is bacteria). Look like bacteria but are a distinct lineage; eukaryotes likely arose from an archaeal host. *(T0)*

**ATP (adenosine triphosphate)** — The cell's energy *currency*, not storage. Splitting off a phosphate (ATP → ADP + Pi) releases ~20 k_BT under cellular conditions. Useful because the cell keeps the ATP/ADP ratio far from equilibrium. A human recycles roughly their body weight in ATP daily. *(T0)*

**ATP synthase** — A rotary motor in the mitochondrial inner membrane. Protons flowing through it spin it, and the rotation makes ATP. *(T0)*

**Bacteria** — Prokaryotes; single cells with no nucleus, typically 1–2 µm. *(T0)*

**Base pairing (complementary)** — A pairs with T (U in RNA), G pairs with C. Each DNA strand therefore determines the other, which is the basis of copying. *(T0)*

**Brown fat** — Tissue that deliberately leaks protons across mitochondrial membranes (via the protein thermogenin) to turn food energy into heat. *(T0)*

**Catalyst** — Something that speeds a reaction without being used up. Speeds forward and reverse equally, so it changes rate, not the final equilibrium. *(T0)*

**Central dogma** — Crick (1958). Narrow, original claim: sequence information never flows back from protein to nucleic acid [consensus, still holds]. Popular loose reading: "DNA is the program, everything else executes it" — this is what Ball and Noble attack [Ball]. Keep the two apart. *(T0, T1)*

**Chaperone** — A protein that helps other proteins fold correctly or prevents misfolding. *(T0, T2)*

**Chemiosmosis** — Making ATP using a proton gradient across a membrane. Mitchell, 1961; ridiculed, then Nobel 1978. [consensus] Used by essentially all life. *(T0)*

**Codon** — Three consecutive letters of mRNA, read by the ribosome as one unit. 4³ = 64 codons. *(T0)*

**Cytoplasm** — The cell's interior outside the nucleus; crowded, not a dilute soup. *(T0)*

**Cytoskeleton** — The internal protein scaffolding of eukaryotic cells; gives shape and carries out active transport. *(T0)*

**Diffusion** — Random thermal motion of molecules. Time to cover distance L scales as t ~ L²/(2dD) (d = dimensions, D = diffusion coefficient): fast over nanometers, hopeless over centimeters. *(T0, T3)*

**DNA** — Deoxyribonucleic acid. A double-stranded helical polymer of nucleotides; stores hereditary sequence. 2 bits per base pair; human genome ≈ 3.1 × 10⁹ bp. *(T0)*

**Electron transport chain** — Membrane proteins in mitochondria that pass electrons from food molecules to oxygen, using the energy to pump protons across the membrane. *(T0)*

**Endosymbiosis** — One cell living inside another. Mitochondria descend from engulfed bacteria. [consensus] *(T0)*

**Enzyme** — A biological catalyst, usually a protein (sometimes RNA — see ribozyme). Rate enhancements ~10⁶ to ~10¹⁷. *(T0)*

**Eukaryote** — Cell with a nucleus and internal membrane compartments (animals, plants, fungi, protists). Typically 10–30 µm. *(T0)*

**Exon** — Part of a gene's RNA that is kept after splicing. *(T0, T1)*

**Gene** — No single agreed definition. Molecular sense: a stretch of DNA that is transcribed into a functional product. Evolutionary sense: a unit inherited and selected over generations (Dawkins' usage). "A sequence that codes for a protein" is inaccurate: misses non-coding genes, alternative splicing, and regulatory DNA. [consensus that the simple definition fails] *(T1)*

**Genetic code** — The many-to-one map from 64 codons to 20 amino acids plus stop. Redundant, and arranged so single-letter errors are often harmless. *(T0, T5)*

**Glycolysis** — First stage of breaking down glucose, in the cytoplasm; yields a little ATP without oxygen. *(T0)*

**Hydrophilic / hydrophobic** — "Water-loving" (polar or charged; interacts favorably with water) / "water-fearing" (nonpolar). About interaction, not chemical reaction. *(T0)*

**Hydrophobic effect** — Nonpolar molecules cluster in water mainly because it frees water molecules from ordered cages — an entropy gain. Drives membrane formation and protein folding. *(T0)*

**Induced fit** — An enzyme changes shape as it binds its substrate (Koshland). Refines lock-and-key. *(T0, T2)*

**Intrinsically disordered protein** — A protein (or region) that has no single fixed fold. *(T2)*

**Intron** — Part of a gene's RNA that is cut out during splicing. *(T0, T1)*

**Kinetic proofreading** — Hopfield (1974). Copying machinery spends energy to recheck and reject errors, reaching accuracy far beyond what binding energies alone allow (DNA copying ~1 error per 10⁹ bp). *(T0, T3)*

**Lipid bilayer** — Two sheets of phospholipids, tails inward, heads facing water on both sides. Self-assembles. Nearly impermeable to ions because they'd have to enter the nonpolar core. *(T0)*

**Lock and key** — Fischer's picture of enzyme specificity: rigid complementary shapes. Superseded in part by induced fit; Ball argues recognition is looser still [Ball]. *(T0, T2)*

**Macromolecular crowding** — Large molecules occupy ~20–30% of cytoplasm volume. *(T0, T2)*

**Membrane protein** — Protein embedded in a membrane: channels (let ions through), pumps (push ions against gradients), receptors (detect signals). About half a membrane's mass. *(T0, T3)*

**Messenger RNA (mRNA)** — The processed RNA copy of a gene that leaves the nucleus and is translated by ribosomes. *(T0)*

**Michaelis–Menten kinetics** — Standard model of enzyme rate: v = V_max[S] / (K_M + [S]). Rises with substrate, then saturates. *(T0, T3)*

**Mitochondrion** — Eukaryotic organelle, descended from a bacterium, where most ATP is made via chemiosmosis. Lane argues mitochondria made complex cells possible by vastly raising energy per gene [research]. *(T0)*

**Non-coding RNA** — RNA that functions as RNA and is never translated: tRNA, ribosomal RNA, microRNAs, long non-coding RNAs. *(T1)*

**Nucleotide** — Building block of DNA/RNA: a sugar, a phosphate, and one base (A, T/U, G, C). *(T0)*

**Nucleus** — Membrane-bound compartment holding a eukaryote's DNA. Separates transcription from translation, enabling RNA processing. *(T0)*

**Phospholipid** — Membrane molecule with a charged/polar head and two hydrocarbon tails. *(T0)*

**Pre-mRNA** — The raw RNA transcript before splicing and capping. *(T0)*

**Prokaryote** — Cell with no nucleus: bacteria and archaea. *(T0)*

**Promoter** — DNA region where RNA polymerase binds to start transcription. *(T0, T1)*

**Protein** — A chain of amino acids that folds into a functional shape. *(T0)*

**Proton gradient (proton-motive force)** — Difference in proton concentration and voltage across a membrane (~150–200 mV across ~5 nm in mitochondria). Powers ATP synthase. *(T0)*

**Ribosome** — Large RNA–protein machine that translates mRNA into protein. Its catalytic core is RNA (a ribozyme). *(T0)*

**Ribozyme** — An RNA molecule that acts as an enzyme. *(T0)*

**RNA** — Ribonucleic acid. Usually single-stranded; uses U instead of T. Carries messages, does catalysis, regulates genes. *(T0, T1)*

**RNA polymerase** — Enzyme that builds an RNA copy of a DNA strand. *(T0)*

**RNA world hypothesis** — Early life used RNA for both information and catalysis before DNA and proteins. [research] — widely favored, unproven. *(T0)*

**Semiconservative replication** — Each new DNA double helix has one old strand and one new strand. Meselson–Stahl, 1958. [consensus] *(T0)*

**Splice site** — Short signal sequences marking intron boundaries. Mutations here can cause intron retention or exon skipping. *(T0, T1)*

**Splicing** — Cutting introns out of pre-mRNA and joining exons. *(T0, T1)*

**Substrate** — The molecule an enzyme acts on. *(T0)*

**Transcription** — Making an RNA copy of a DNA sequence, by RNA polymerase, in the nucleus (in eukaryotes). *(T0)*

**Transcription factor** — Protein that binds DNA and helps control whether a gene is transcribed. *(T0, T1)*

**Transfer RNA (tRNA)** — Adapter RNA carrying one amino acid and recognizing the matching codon. *(T0)*

**Transition state** — The unstable halfway configuration at the top of the activation barrier. Enzymes work by binding it more tightly than the substrate (Pauling). *(T0)*

**Translation** — Building a protein from mRNA, by ribosomes, codon by codon. *(T0)*

**Uncoupling** — Letting protons leak back across the membrane without passing through ATP synthase; food energy becomes heat instead of ATP. *(T0)*
